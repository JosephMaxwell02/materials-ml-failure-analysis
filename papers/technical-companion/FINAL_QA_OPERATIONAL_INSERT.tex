\section{Scientific units, fits, and accepted boundaries}
\callout{Scientific unit.}{The smallest contract-bound part of a declared experiment that may complete independently. It binds scientific coordinates, required outputs, and execution identity; it commits only after output, schema, identity, and atomic-commit checks pass.}

A unit is not a fixed quantity of computation. Matbench V0.2 makes the distinction concrete: 20 official-fold units each contained one baseline fit and five target-permutation refits, so \textbf{20 units represented 120 estimator fits}. Matbench V0.3 contained 20 baseline/perturbation, 2,840 individual-removal, and 580 group-removal units per identity. Spatial V0.6.1 contained 144 valid and 432 paired-null units per identity.

\begin{center}\small\bfseries
DECLARED UNIT $\rightarrow$ LOCAL STAGING $\rightarrow$ FILE/SCHEMA/IDENTITY/HASH CHECKS $\rightarrow$ ATOMIC COMMIT $\rightarrow$ JOURNAL/CHECKPOINT
\end{center}
Invalid or incomplete staging is retained as failure evidence and recomputed; it does not advance the checkpoint. \textbf{Resume from the last verified scientific boundary, not from the last visible file.}

\section{Observed continuity on a shared workstation}
The JARVIS replay was interrupted and resumed under the same identity; incomplete staging was rejected and recomputed. The Materials Project replay similarly retained only verified complete units across a later resume launch. A controlled-fixture matrix terminated child processes at 11 declared fault boundaries and recovered each fixture to three unique units. The latter is controller fault-injection evidence, not 11 scientific experiments.

The principal workstation was shared rather than dedicated. Calendar time included ordinary use, review, interruption, and unknown gaps. It must not be reported as active runtime. Elapsed process time, summed unit wall time, committed-unit span, and calendar elapsed are different quantities. Spatial V0.6.1's 20.67 h fresh and 16.20 h replay values are committed-unit spans.

\begin{center}\small\ttfamily
[RUN] -- [ordinary use/review] -- [RUN] -- [interrupt/checkpoint] -- [RESUME] -- [analysis] -- [REPLAY]
\end{center}

\callout{Boundary.}{Resume tests continuation of one identity. Repeat creates a separately identified same-environment execution where applicable. Replay links a new identity to a predecessor under declared comparison contracts. Resume is not replication, and replay is not automatically external replication.}

The furnace route varied geometry, aperture, emissivity, contact and seal assumptions, hot-side boundary, cooling loss, and edge/frame transfer. The tank route separated source provenance, residence, return, gate, consumed, and contained states; its descendant improved an exploratory worst service margin from $-0.266$ to $-0.090$ without resolving final sizing. The heat-shield monolith produced a 175.3--389.5 K nominal reduction range, whereas the segmented descendant included $-83.5$ to 245.4 K while improving local fault information. These are bounded screening instruments, not physical validation.

The technical contribution is the programme's design synthesis and engineering reduction, not a claim of literature-level novelty for every physical mechanism.
